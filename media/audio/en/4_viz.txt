The object “The Trading Rows” is dedicated to the commercial life of Hadiach and to the role of Jewish residents in the local economy. Trade in the small town was not only an exchange of goods for money; it created networks of contacts between producers, carriers, middlemen, buyers, and other towns and regions. In the research, Jewish presence appears in various kinds of economic activity: trade, transport, brokerage and the supply of goods.

The visual image of the trading object consists of a cart, containers, crockery and other items that refer to transport and exchange. It is a generalised artistic composition, not an exact reproduction of a particular merchant or trade route. Its task is to show movement and interdependence: goods arrived, were stored, sold, passed from hand to hand, and connected the town with the wider world.

The name “Shuk” is a Hebrew word meaning “market”. In the project it is used as a conceptual title, not as a claim that the trading rows of Hadiach were historically called by this name. A separate and important link leads to indigo and to a local shirt known in the Hadiach region by its historical name, “zhydivka”. The research materials speak of a trade in blue threads and indigo dye associated with Jewish merchants. This material route — from goods to thread, from thread to embroidery and clothing — shows how trade could influence local culture and everyday life.
