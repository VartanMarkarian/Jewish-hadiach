The object “The Former Synagogue” was created for a point connected with the former synagogue of Hadiach. Today the building houses the Molotok museum, and its present appearance does not necessarily allow a visitor to guess its former function. At the same time, the archival photographs on which the research relies give grounds to identify the building as a synagogue.

The digital sculpture brings into the present-day space the image of the Aron ha-Kodesh — the holy ark, a cabinet or niche in which the Torah scrolls are kept. It is one of the principal sacred elements of the synagogue hall. The ark is not an ordinary piece of furniture: it marks the place where a text of central importance to Judaism and to synagogue worship is kept. In front of the Aron ha-Kodesh there is usually a parochet — a special textile curtain that covers its doors or opening. A parochet could be made of costly fabrics, such as velvet or brocade, and decorated with embroidery, appliqué, ornament and fringe. Its function is not merely decorative: the curtain emphasises the special status of the place behind which the Torah scrolls are kept. Inside the ark is the Sefer Torah — a handwritten scroll of the Five Books of Moses, written on parchment and attached to two wooden rollers. The scroll is used for public reading. During the research it became clear that it is important to distinguish between “Torah” as the sacred text and teaching, and “Sefer Torah” as a specific handwritten scroll.

In the project, the parochet and the Aron ha-Kodesh play a special role: they bring into a contemporary architectural space objects and forms that are not typical of a familiar secular Ukrainian interior and that, without explanation, might remain incomprehensible. Through them, the visitor learns that this building was not simply a public hall, but a sacred place of the Jewish community.
