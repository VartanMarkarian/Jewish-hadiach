The object “A Place at the Desk” is dedicated to Jewish children and their presence in the educational life of Hadiach. Its starting point is a school location connected with School Number Two. In the materials available to the author, no photographs have yet been found of Jewish children who studied at this particular school; at the same time, local history accounts and references in research indicate that Jewish children studied within the town’s educational environment.

The digital object takes the form of an old school desk. The desk is deliberately generalised: such a model could have stood in many different schools, and it has no specially constructed features of a Jewish educational institution. It is precisely this ordinariness that matters to the concept, because Jewish children were part of the town’s society and its educational space, yet today their presence cannot always be seen or recovered from photographs. A Star of David has been scratched by hand into the surface of the desk. This authorial gesture marks a hidden or forgotten identity: a small trace on the wood becomes a sign of presence that does not turn into a loud declaration.

The name “Limud” comes from the Hebrew word meaning “learning” or “study”. During the research it became clear that, in terms of terminology, it is important to distinguish between the cheder — a traditional Jewish elementary religious school; the Talmud Torah — an educational institution or communal initiative for religious learning; and the yeshiva — an institution for the advanced study of religious texts. The object speaks of the right to be part of a shared space of learning while preserving one’s own memory. It does not claim that all Jewish children had to hide their origins, or that at a particular school they were forbidden to call themselves Jews. Instead, the artistic metaphor focuses on the absence of visible traces: the desk remains, while the stories of the children who may have studied at it have to be pieced together from fragments.
