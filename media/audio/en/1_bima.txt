The object “The Synagogue That No Longer Exists” reflects on the synagogue as an important spiritual and communal centre of Jewish Hadiach. The name of this point speaks of architectural absence, yet the digital sculpture turns our attention back to the inner organisation of such a space and to its functions.

The central element of the composition is a raised wooden platform with steps, a railing and an arched structure. It refers to the bimah — the raised place in a synagogue intended for the public reading of the Torah. The bimah could have a table or a special surface on which the scroll was laid. Its architectural prominence emphasised the importance of reading the sacred text in the life of the community. A lamp is also present in the composition. In Jewish synagogue tradition there is the concept of the ner tamid — the “constant”, or “eternal”, light, which is usually placed near the Aron ha-Kodesh. In the context of this composition, it is important to distinguish between the bimah and the amud: the bimah is connected above all with the reading of the Torah, while the amud is the prayer lectern, the place where the hazzan stands while leading the prayer. These elements may coexist in a synagogue space, but they are not interchangeable.

The arches, steps, railings and elevation of the digital object are not presented as a precise archaeological reconstruction of the Hadiach synagogue. This is the author’s architectural interpretation, which gathers recognisable features of a sacred interior into a single image. The object invites us to imagine not only the building, but also the practices that took place inside it: prayer, reading, gathering, the passing on of knowledge and the sustaining of the community.
