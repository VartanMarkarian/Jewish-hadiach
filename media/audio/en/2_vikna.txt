The object “Life of the Jewish Quarter” is dedicated to the life of the Jewish quarter as part of the shared urban space of Hadiach. It is based on archival photographs of the town: houses, streets, horses, transport and people. These images do not necessarily make it possible to determine the religious or ethnic background of particular residents. They capture a shared everyday life in which different communities lived side by side and were woven into one urban fabric. The visual solution is built on contrast: the archival town is rendered in black and white, while a single window remains lit. Inside it are books and a menorah. This artistic image does not claim that a particular Jewish family lived in the house depicted, nor is it a reconstruction of a real interior. The lit window is the author’s sign of a presence that is not always visible in a documentary image.

The menorah and the books carry different meanings. The menorah is a recognisable symbol of Jewish religious and cultural tradition; in its narrow historical sense, the menorah is the seven-branched lamp associated with the Tabernacle and the Temple in Jerusalem. The books refer to education, reading, religious texts and cultural memory.

The name “Kehillah” comes from the Hebrew word for community. In the extended description, it helps us speak of the Jewish population not as an isolated group, but as a community with its own family, religious, educational and economic ties — one that was, at the same time, part of Hadiach.
