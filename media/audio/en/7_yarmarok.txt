“Threads of Shared Memory” is the final object of the route, gathering the previous themes into a single spatial metaphor. Its foundation is the idea of connections: between people, crafts, trade, objects, ornaments and the memory of the town. The Jewish history of Hadiach appears here not as an isolated fragment, but as part of the wider history of the town. The visual composition combines archival photographs of Hadiach placed on burlap, interwoven threads, ornamental surfaces, and a fragment based on an original traditional shirt known in the Hadiach region by its historical name, “zhydivka”.

A special place is given to the indigo thread — indigo being the blue dye used to colour textile fibres. In the research materials, the trade in blue threads and dye is associated with Jewish merchants, and the local “zhydivka” shirt with its characteristic blue or blue-violet embroidery. In this way, a garment becomes material evidence of the interaction between a trading presence and the local Ukrainian textile tradition. The composition uses not an arbitrary stylisation, but a fragment based on an original shirt. The threads in the composition work quite literally as material and structure, joining separate parts together; at the same time, they are a metaphor for historical connections that may have been broken, forgotten or no longer obvious, yet continue to show through in things, photographs, crafts and local traditions. The burlap gives the image materiality and an archival quality: memory here is not abstract — it is kept in fabric, fibre, image and object.

The final object brings the route to a close with the thought that present-day Hadiach has been shaped by many interwoven histories. The Jewish community was one of the important strands of this urban fabric, and its contribution calls not for a decorative mention, but for attentive research, reflection and rethinking.
