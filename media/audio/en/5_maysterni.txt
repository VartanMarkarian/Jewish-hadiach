“The Matter of Labour” reveals Jewish presence in Hadiach through crafts and trades. Against a narrow notion of the Jewish community as an exclusively commercial one, this object sets a broader picture of everyday work. The small town needed people who processed leather, delivered water, sewed and mended clothes, worked with various materials, and made and repaired things.

Particularly important in the concept are leather dressers and tanners, water carriers, tailors, shoemakers and other craftsmen. Leather dressers and tanners both worked with leather, but these names should not be mechanically equated: tanning is above all associated with curing and processing raw hides. Water carriers delivered water to homes, workshops and shops. Tailors made, altered and mended clothing.

The visual composition gathers the tools of labour into a kind of workshop archive: a Singer sewing machine, fabric, scissors, tools, a vessel and a small bowl of paint or powder. The object speaks of labour as the foundation of urban society. A town is shaped not only by architecture, trade or administrative decisions, but also by the daily actions of people who make, repair, deliver and maintain the things and services it needs. The Jewish community was one of those communities whose labour took part in shaping Hadiach.
